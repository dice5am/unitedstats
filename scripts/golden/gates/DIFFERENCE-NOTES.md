# How it's calculated: source difference notes (one line per card)

- **#3 Living-standards league table:** This chart uses OECD GDP per person at current PPPs. The IMF's version (WEO) is close for 2025, between 3.5% lower (France) and 3.2% higher (Germany), but it tells a different long-run story: Germany grew 292% since 1990 in OECD data and 235% in IMF data. The two use different PPP estimates and data vintages, so ranks less than about 3% apart should be read as close.
- **#7 The great rate hike:** This chart uses the BIS series of central bank policy rates (end of month). The OECD's short-term "immediate" interest rate is a different measure. In September 2026 it sat between 0.01 points (Canada) and 0.23 points (euro area) below the policy rate, and it rose 2.85 points in the euro area since January 2022 against 2.50 for the policy rate.
- **#10 Public debt:** This chart uses IMF WEO general government gross debt. OECD's quarterly government debt table is compiled differently and gives higher figures for some countries: UK 150.7% of GDP against 102.3% (2025), US 136.3% against 123.9%, Canada 120.0% against 110.0% (2024). Germany, Italy and Japan are within 0.4 points.
- **#14 Emissions since 1990:** This chart uses OECD totals from the greenhouse-gas inventories countries report to the UN. The World Bank's EDGAR estimates, modelled by the EU's Joint Research Centre, are within about 4% for most countries but put Canada 11% higher in 2024 (768 against 685 Mt) and its rise since 1990 at 30% rather than 13%.
- **#22 Jobs recovery scorecard:** This chart uses OECD employment rates for ages 15-64. ILOSTAT's rates for the same age group are within 0.8 points on the latest level, but the change since 2019-Q4 differs: Germany +2.6 points in OECD data against +0.2 in ILO data, and France +2.3 against +3.3. OECD flags Germany's recent quarters as estimates.
- **#25 How unequal are incomes:** This chart uses the OECD Income Distribution Database Gini (shown ×100). The World Bank's Poverty and Inequality Platform builds its figures from different underlying surveys and income definitions, and its Gini differs by up to 3 points: UK 32.4 against 35.4 (2021), Germany 33.7 against 30.9 (2022), US 41.8 against 39.4 (2023).
